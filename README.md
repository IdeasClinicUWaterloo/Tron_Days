# Welcome to Tron Days!

This event is hosted by the [University of Waterloo Pearl Sullivan Engineering IDEAs Clinic](https://uwaterloo.ca/engineering-ideas-clinic/).

In many modern songs, we hear sounds that are not recognizable as traditional instruments such as the guitar, piano, violin, or drums.
These sounds are created by electrical musical instruments called **synthesizers**, or a software imitation thereof!

As their name might suggest, most of the first synthesizers were originally made with the goal of artificially reproducing (or *synthesizing*) the sounds of acoustic instruments like those listed above.
Over the decades, they have evolved into their own category of instrument with their own distinct sounds, rather than trying to imitate acoustic instruments.

Analog synthesizers typically create sounds by generating waveforms through methods including subtractive synthesis, additive synthesis, and frequency modulation (FM) synthesis.
These sounds may be altered by components such as:

- **filters**, which cut or boost frequencies;
- **envelopes**, which control articulation, or how notes begin and end;
- **low-frequency oscillators (LFOs)**, which modulate parameters such as pitch, volume, or tone.

Over two days, you will explore the mathematics of sound, design and build a cardboard speaker cabinet, and then assemble and program your own synthesizer.
